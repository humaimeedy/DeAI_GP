# DeAI_GP

Supporting review data for **Public Good in Decentralized Artificial Intelligence: Foundations, Evidence and Evaluation**.

The workbook `full_final_corpus_content_quality.xlsx` provides the study-level evidence behind the review and DeAI-PG framework. This version contains **353 main studies: 326 General AI and 27 DeAI-related studies**, accompanied by **four linked publications**, giving 357 retained publications overall. The publication window is 1 January 2023 to 13 November 2026. This dataset version is dated 21 November 2026.

## Using the workbook

| Worksheet | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Review status | Corpus scope, counting rules and interpretation of the evidence |
| Study evidence map | Compact RQ1 and RQ2 accounts for all 353 main studies, including concern codes, native measurement specifications, evidence classification and source locations; this preserves the study-level map previously presented in Appendix B |
| Coding key | The 27 RQ1 concern codes, their coding rules and the 12 RQ2 measurement classifications |
| Corpus | Publication identities, discovery provenance, document and reading-status records |
| Complete extraction | Native extraction fields covering AI context, public contribution, assessment object, measurements and supporting evidence |
| Study coding | Non-exclusive concern, domain and measurement codes for the 353 main studies |
| Results frequencies | Formula-based frequencies and percentages using the corresponding corpus denominators |
| Quality summary, Quality matrix, Quality judgments and Quality rubric | Appraisal criteria, all 353 profiles, 1,765 evidence-supported judgments and formula-based descriptive statistics |
| References | Full references as supplied, canonical identifiers and complete manuscript BibTeX records for all main studies |
| Linked publications, Linked report extraction, Linked report appraisals and Linked report judgments | Companion-report relationships and available additional extraction/appraisal records, kept outside the main-study denominator |
| Evidence provenance | Selected overlaps, documentary sources and illustrations relevant to interpreting the retained evidence |
| Decisions and Language exclusions | Eligibility and reconciliation decisions, including the language-based exclusion |

Stable study and record identifiers are retained. Gaps in study numbering do not represent missing included rows.

## Interpretation

The 353 studies supply 3,271 non-exclusive study–concern assignments. A study is counted once within each concern and once within a domain when it addresses at least one constituent concern. Frequencies describe this corpus and do not determine normative weights.

RQ1 coding identifies substantive concerns and their analytical relationship to public good. A positive code does not imply that the publication explicitly uses the phrase “public good.” RQ2 classes describe assessment approaches and the objects they measure. Native units, formulas, scale anchors, evidence requirements and interpretive limits are retained where recorded. A blank field in the compact map means that no separate entry appeared in that map; the complete extraction retains the source-specific qualifications.

There are 116 studies classified as applying assessment to observed data or real practices and 32 reporting reliability or validity analysis. These counts do not represent independent trials of public-good outcomes or validated instruments for public good as a complete construct.

Quality appraisal uses five criteria scored 0, 0.5 or 1, with a maximum total of five. The 353 profiles contain 1,765 criterion judgments, with mean 4.662889518414, median 5 and range 2.5–5. No high-, medium- or low-quality bands are assigned. The judgments retain their rationales, supporting extraction evidence, source locations and uncertainties. Appraisal used retained extractions, earlier verification and targeted primary checks; this version does not represent a new full-text reread of every publication or independent human dual coding. Methodological quality, evidence type and demonstrated public-good contribution remain distinct.

The five public-evidence applications in the manuscript use separate public project sources. They are not additions to the 353-study review corpus. Full-text publications are not distributed in this repository.
